The Story of Pleasant Valley School

More than a century of education, preservation, and community.

ESTABLISHED IN 1899

A Schoolhouse That Stood the Test of Time

Pleasant Valley School was built in 1899 on land homesteaded by the Zuck family in Payne County, Oklahoma. The one-room schoolhouse served local children for more than four decades, until classes ended in 1941.

In the 1980s, the historic building faced demolition when plans for a city electrical substation threatened the property. Community members rallied to protect the schoolhouse, establishing the Pleasant Valley School Foundation and raising funds for its restoration.

Restoration work was completed in May 1988. The project included repairs to the building, historically appropriate furnishings, two outhouses, and the return of the original school bell from Colorado.

On October 29, 1988, Pleasant Valley School was rededicated, beginning a new chapter as a living history destination.

LIVING HISTORY

Experience Education From Another Era

Today, Pleasant Valley School helps younger generations discover what classroom life was like in Oklahoma more than a century ago.

Students can practice penmanship with quill pens and ink, read from McGuffey's Readers, and work through arithmetic lessons using traditional slates.

Outside the classroom, children experience old-fashioned recess activities such as sack races and hoop rolling. These experiences connect students with the everyday lives of earlier generations.
